## Independent oracle results

The standard-library Python oracle constructs finite complex LFM samples and
evaluates the same zero-filled ambiguity sum independently of MATLAB source
execution.

- Every tested nonzero waveform has normalized origin magnitude 1. The LFM
  zero-Doppler magnitude is even in delay, and the -120/+120 kHz ridge peaks
  at negative/positive delay under the declared convention.
- A nontrivial complex waveform satisfies the auto-ambiguity magnitude
  symmetry `|chi(tau,nu)| = |chi(-tau,-nu)|` at all 45 cells on a symmetric
  delay-Doppler grid. This exercises simultaneous mismatch, negative-delay
  indexing, conjugation, and Doppler sign together rather than checking only
  axis cuts.
- Rectangular durations `[6.5 13 26]` microseconds give full -3 dB delay widths
  `[3.807612 7.615224 15.230447]` microseconds and Doppler widths
  `[136.300317 68.077965 33.977302]` kHz.
- LFM bandwidths `[1.5 3 4.5]` MHz give zero-Doppler full -3 dB delay widths
  `[0.583922 0.285135 0.193594]` microseconds. At +120 kHz their sampled ridge
  delays are `[1.0 0.5 0.3]` microseconds, consistent with `tau=nu/(B/T)` to
  the 0.1-microsecond delay grid.
- At the extreme 129-sample shift, correct rectangular overlap is
  `0.007692307692` (`1/130`); circular wrap is `1.0`; restoring zero filling
  exactly restores the correct value.
- Zero-delay Doppler cuts for 7-, 13-, and 31-chip equal-amplitude signals
  narrow monotonically with total duration. This check is pattern-independent;
  the module deliberately does not claim monotonic phase-code sidelobes.
- Negative cases reject missing/empty/duplicate/malformed identity data,
  Boolean or wrong seeds, NaN/infinite/nonpositive controls, even Doppler-bin
  counts, Nyquist violations, baseline omissions, unordered or fractional
  sweeps, inconsistent pulse/code durations, every represented resource
  ceiling, empty/zero/nonfinite signals, out-of-range delays, and nonfinite
  Doppler inputs.

The Python oracle does not reproduce MATLAB's `RandStream` samples and makes
no claim about the exact seeded polarity vector or rendered MATLAB phase-code
surface.
