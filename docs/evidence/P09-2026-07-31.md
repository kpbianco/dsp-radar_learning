## Independent numerical contracts

`tests/test_p09_module.py` independently reconstructs the FIR coefficients,
IIR bilinear-transform coefficients, second-order poles, complex frequency
response, group delay, direct difference equation, step metrics, and bounded
broken/recovery response in Python. These checks do not substitute for MATLAB
runtime evidence.

- The 21-tap FIR is symmetric to floating-point tolerance, has DC gain one,
  exact 10-sample group delay at checked non-null frequencies, and exactly zero
  output after its last tap for an impulse.
- The second-order Butterworth IIR has DC gain one, maximum pole radius
  `0.642496380`, and a decaying theoretically infinite impulse response.
- On the committed 1025-point grid, both filters first cross minus three
  decibels at `100.097656250 Hz`. This establishes comparable cutoff, not equal
  stopbands: at 250 Hz the FIR is `-50.893417133 dB` and the IIR is
  `-19.577095499 dB`.
- FIR group delay is `10 samples`; IIR group delay changes from
  `2.710797583 samples` at 60 Hz to `0.502417412 samples` at 250 Hz.
- The independent step model reports FIR/IIR overshoot of
  `4.857460484%`/`4.975291945%` and two-percent settling after onset of
  `16`/`9 samples`.
- Tap-count cases independently retain delays `4`, `10`, and `20 samples`.
- Q cases `0.5`, `0.707106781`, and `2` have increasing pole radii
  `0.509525457`, `0.642496380`, and `0.862415667`, with increasing overshoot
  `0%`, `4.975291945%`, and `43.709458275%`.
- The finite 160-sample broken radius-1.02 impulse has late/early RMS ratio
  `12.293176676`; recovery at radius 0.98 has ratio `0.072392982`. A separate
  guard rejects the unstable denominator when stable execution is required.
