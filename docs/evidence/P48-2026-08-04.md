## Physical model and independent static oracle

The experiment uses independent exponential square-law background power. Mean
power is one below range cell 121 and `10^(12/10)` at and above it. For every
eligible CUT, 12 lower-index and 12 higher-index training cells are separated
from the CUT by two guards per side. GO selects the larger one-sided arithmetic
mean; SO selects the smaller. Baseline target probes use background-only
reference estimates so the four probe locations do not contaminate each other;
the second sweep introduces exactly one training-cell interferer explicitly.

For `T = 12` and homogeneous `Pfa = 1e-3`, an independent Python
implementation of the finite-sum probability and fixed bisection gives:

- calibrated GO scale: `7.089038451813456`;
- ordinary 24-cell CA scale used by the broken comparison:
  `8.004514371919775`;
- calibrated SO scale: `10.480855745946773`;
- broken shared-scale GO `Pfa`: `0.0004729226614182537`; and
- broken shared-scale SO `Pfa`: `0.0038688671274318283`.

A separate 30,000-trial seeded Python oracle, which does not reproduce
MATLAB's sample stream, measured paired high-side edge `(GO, SO)` false-alarm
probabilities of approximately `(0.00123, 0.00113)` at 0 dB and
`(0.00477, 0.84770)` at 18 dB. At a 13 dB weak CUT with a 20 dB excess-power
interferer in one training cell, empirical `(GO, SO)` detection probabilities
were approximately `(0.000, 0.939)`. The added low-side edge-target regression
measured paired `(GO, SO)` detection probabilities of approximately
`(0.983, 0.976)` at 0 dB contrast and `(0.000, 0.940)` at 18 dB contrast. These
checks support both sides of the governing tradeoff: SO's high-side false-alarm
cost and GO's low-side masking cost. They are simulated mathematical evidence,
not MATLAB execution, sample-for-sample MATLAB results, rare-event `Pfa`
validation, or radar-system evidence.
