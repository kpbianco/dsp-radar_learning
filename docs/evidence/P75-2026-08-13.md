## Deterministic simulated-oracle results

Permanent Python standard-library tests independently mirror the reviewed
geometry, two-way carrier phase, quadratic curvature, private noise generator,
aperture subsets, candidate-path coherent sum, failure, and recovery. They do
not consume MATLAB output.

For `c = 3e8 m/s`, `f_c = 5 GHz`, `lambda = 0.06 m`, closest range `1000 m`,
an 80 m aperture, and `0.2 m` spatial spacing:

- the aperture has 401 spatial samples, the focus grid has 209 candidates from
  -26 to +26 m, and the fast-time range axis has 201
  samples from 995 to 1005 m;
- edge-to-center one-way range excursion is `0.799680255744 m`, giving
  `26.6560085248` round-trip phase turns and `5.331201705 ns` delay span;
- the numerical phase curvature at closest approach agrees with
  `-4*pi/(lambda*R0)` within the permanent tolerance;
- target coordinates `-20`, `0`, and `+20 m` place the exact range/phase vertex
  at the same platform coordinates; the symmetric targets have equal slant
  range and phase at platform center but differ away from center;
- centered 20, 40, and 80 m subsets contain 101, 201, and 401 looks and span
  `1.66662500208`, `6.66600013330`, and `26.6560085248` phase turns;
- the private seed's first unit-variance complex draw is
  `-0.857595287099 - j1.44905160405`, repeats exactly at seed 7501, and differs
  at seed 7601; and
- on the clean aperture oracle, magnitude-only processing has best normalized
  coherent score below `0.08`, while unchanged complex I/Q focuses at exactly
  `0 m` with score `1.0` and remains byte-for-byte equal as Python values.

The permanent full-matrix oracle also reproduces the Gaussian envelope,
MATLAB column-major noise placement, nearest-range-bin ridge extraction, and
reviewed 30 dB noise. It constructs and repeats all `401 x 201` complex
samples, checks selected cells equal clean signal plus additive noise, and
places the clean center and aperture-edge ridges at range-bin indices `100`
and `116`, respectively. The magnitude-only best coordinate/score is
`24.0 m / 0.0741407`, while unchanged complex recovery is
`0.0 m / 0.9997667`. This is a host-language test result, not MATLAB runtime
evidence.

The completion behavior is also exercised with the same full noisy model for
targets at `-20 m` and `+20 m` cross-range. Both retain `1000 m` closest range,
their aperture measurements differ, and the coherent score peaks at their
distinct true coordinates with scores `0.9997389` and `0.9997600`.
