shifted-copy addition, and the broken overwrite rule in Python. These checks do
not substitute for MATLAB runtime evidence.

  `[0, 5, 9]` produces a 49-sample full linear output. Three independently
  shifted/scaled rows exactly equal independent full convolution.
- The individual copy-center samples are `[8, 13, 17]`. At `n=14`, independent
  contributions `[0, 0.45, -0.0875] V` sum to `0.3625 V`.
- Two asymmetric signed inputs, mixed with unequal signed scale factors, verify
  superposition for a separate three-path response. Every mixed-output sample
  equals both independent full convolution and the sum of its three visible
  path contributions; reversing the asymmetric input produces a different
  output, so the fixture detects an accidental correlation/reversal convention.

  output equals the sum of valid `h[k]x[n-k]` products for that frame.

  cases retain third-path support and change signed contribution size.
- An accepted all-negative edited pulse verifies the gain-reversal contract is
  polarity-independent: the `-0.70` contribution is `-2` times the `+0.35`
  contribution.
- The fixed `[0,3,6]` broken fixture gives an independent `1.000000 V` maximum
  overwrite error across seven samples. Restoring addition gives zero
