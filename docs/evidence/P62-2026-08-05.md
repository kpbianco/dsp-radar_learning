## Deterministic simulated-oracle results

The independent Python oracle uses only the standard library and separately
implements the private Park–Miller record, complex ULA sum, normalization,
crossing interpolation, first-null/PSL measurement, aperture and spacing
sweeps, taper weights, grating-angle enumeration, and malformed-input checks.

- The seed-6201 uniform record begins
  `(0.048531316010528854, 0.6658242760066987, 0.5086029316338724, 0.0894680575418603)`.
- The uniform `M=8`, `d/lambda=0.5`, broadside response is symmetric and
  normalized to one. On the reviewed `0.025 deg` grid its HPBW is
  `12.802523075064096 deg`, first-null width is `28.95 deg`, and PSL is
  `-12.79735355607447 dB`.
- The element-count sweep has apertures `[1.5, 3.5, 7.5] wavelengths` and
  monotonically decreasing HPBW `[26.3229454, 12.8025231, 6.3587092] deg`.
- At broadside, spacing `[0.5, 0.75, 1.0] wavelengths` narrows the local beam;
  the first two cases have no equal-height visible replica, while the
  one-wavelength case reaches the `+/-90 deg` boundary aliases.
- The explicit eight-point Hamming taper widens HPBW to
  `20.40733114095132 deg` and lowers PSL to `-33.621195652975786 dB`, compared
  with the uniform baseline.
- With one-wavelength spacing and `+30 deg` steering, direct sums at `+30` and
  `-30 deg` both equal one to floating-point precision. Applying the explicit
  Hamming taper leaves both aliased directions at one, so taper does not repair
  their identical sampled phases. Half-wavelength recovery preserves the
  intended value one, predicts no visible grating direction, and reduces the
  old false-direction magnitude below `1e-12`.

These are deterministic simulated-oracle results, not MATLAB runtime,
rendered-figure, physical-array, or operational-radar results.
